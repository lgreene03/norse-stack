number here is regenerated by the code in [research/](../../research) (commands at

## Method

**Source.** data.binance.vision, Binance's public archive (licence CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).
Monthly files only: spot klines, and for USDT-margined perpetuals, funding rates,
premium index klines and (for BTC and ETH) perpetual klines. The archive was listed
through its bucket's public listing: 1,205 prefixes, a few with more than one page.

**Verification.** Every file was downloaded once, checked against the SHA-256 in its
published `.CHECKSUM` and size-capped before use; a mismatch would have stopped the
run (none occurred). The mirror holds 51,756 archive files with their CHECKSUM
files, 957 MB, in the owner's `NORSE_DATASETS_DIR`. The fetcher kept to at most ten
requests a second.

**Periods.** Development is everything up to 2025-12-31; the holdout is 2026-01-01 to
2026-10-31; later months are out of scope. Monthly files never straddle the boundary.

**Gaps.** A gap is a missing bar on the interval's UTC grid, or a funding settlement
missing against the interval each row declares (8, 4, 2 or 1 hours, with a minute of
tolerance for the archive's millisecond jitter). A missing monthly file is listed
separately. Rows the archive publishes twice identically are counted once and listed;
bars off the interval grid are set aside, listed, and the slots they sit in counted as
a gap.

## The candidate instruments

H1's universe is, at each month end, the five largest non-stablecoin Binance USDT spot
pairs by 30-day volume, delisted pairs included. Knowing which pairs that rule can ever
select needs every pair's volume, so the daily klines of the 749 USDT spot pairs that
are not stablecoins or fiat (of 776 the archive lists) were mirrored (27,990 files) and
ranked by quote volume (USDT, the only volume comparable across coins) over the 30 daily
bars closing at each month end. Excluded as stablecoins or fiat, provisionally until
G0.7 fixes the list: AEUR, AUD, BFUSD, BKRW, BUSD, DAI, EURI, EUR, FDUSD, FRAX, GBP,
PAX, RLUSD, RWUSD, SUSD, TUSD, USD1, USDC, USDEB, USDE, USDP, USDSB, USDSOLD, USDS,
USTC, UST and XUSD.

Details that G0.7 still has to fix (the exact stablecoin list, a minimum history, and
whether leveraged tokens, gold tokens and tokenised shares count) would each change who
ranks fifth. So the candidate set is the union of the **top ten** at every month end,
not the top five: any rule that excludes up to five more pairs still selects only from
it. Over the 101 development month ends (2017-08 to 2025-12) that is **102 pairs**, of
which 46 ever reach a top five. The holdout's month ends (2025-12 to 2026-09) add 13
more pairs (marked below). Two ranked pairs are named in Chinese; a tool assuming
ASCII symbols would have dropped them silently, though neither reaches a top ten. BTC
and ETH also serve H2.

How early the rule can run:

| Milestone | First month end |
|---|---|
| Any five USDT pairs to rank | 2017-11 (only BTC and ETH before) |
| Five pairs with 120 daily bars, H1's longest lookback | 2018-03 |
| Every one of the top five has 120 daily bars | 2018-04 |

## Trial caps by the minimum backtest length rule

### The rule

Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado and Zhu ("Pseudo-mathematics and financial
charlatanism", Notices of the AMS 61(5), 2014, pp. 458-471) ask how long a backtest
must be before the best of N configurations with no skill stops looking good. If every
configuration's true annualised Sharpe is zero, its estimate over y years is about
Normal with mean 0 and standard deviation 1/sqrt(y), so the best of N is expected
near E[max Z_N] / sqrt(y), where

    E[max Z_N] ~ (1 - g) * Phi^-1(1 - 1/N) + g * Phi^-1(1 - 1/(N e))

with g = 0.5772 (the Euler-Mascheroni constant) and Phi^-1 the standard normal
quantile. Asking that this expected best stay below a target Sharpe S gives the
minimum backtest length

    MinBTL(N, S) = (E[max Z_N] / S)^2 years,

which the paper bounds above by 2 ln(N) / S^2. Its worked example is the known-answer
test in `research/tests/test_minbtl.py`: MinBTL(45, 1) = 4.998 years, so five years of
data allow no more than 45 configurations. The same file checks the approximation
against simulated maxima of normals and the 1/sqrt(y) scaling against simulated
skill-less strategies.

Two properties make the rule safe to apply here. Under the null the Sharpe is zero, so
the skew and fat tails of crypto returns do not change its standard error to first
order (their terms are multiplied by the Sharpe itself). And trials of one hypothesis
are correlated, so they count as fewer than N independent ones; using the raw trial
count errs on the strict side.

### The target Sharpe

**S = 1.0 annualised.** It is the paper's own benchmark, and it sits below every pass
mark the gate implies (a net Sharpe of about 1.4 at N = 10 and 1.6 at N = 30): with
history at least MinBTL long, noise alone is expected to reach at most 0.63 to 0.71 of
the passing Sharpe, and the deflated Sharpe then does the exact correction with the
ledger's N. A stricter S = 0.75 is shown alongside so G0.7 can see the price of
tightening it.

### The arithmetic

| N | Phi^-1(1 - 1/N) | Phi^-1(1 - 1/(Ne)) | E[max Z_N] = 0.4228 a + 0.5772 b | MinBTL at S = 1 | at S = 0.75 | Bound 2 ln N at S = 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 0.9674 | 1.5438 | 1.3001 | 1.69 years | 3.00 | 3.58 |
| 10 | 1.2816 | 1.7892 | 1.5746 | 2.48 | 4.41 | 4.61 |
| 12 | 1.3830 | 1.8712 | 1.6648 | 2.77 | 4.93 | 4.97 |
| 18 | 1.5932 | 2.0448 | 1.8539 | 3.44 | 6.11 | 5.78 |
| 24 | 1.7317 | 2.1615 | 1.9798 | 3.92 | 6.97 | 6.36 |
| 30 | 1.8339 | 2.2488 | 2.0734 | 4.30 | 7.64 | 6.80 |
| 45 | 2.0099 | 2.4010 | 2.2356 | 5.00 | 8.89 | 7.61 |

For example, at N = 30: 0.4228 x 1.8339 + 0.5772 x 2.2488 = 0.7754 + 1.2980 = 2.0734,
and 2.0734 squared is 4.299 years.

(`norse_research.minbtl.max_trials`):

| Years of history | 3.0 | 3.5 | 4.0 | 4.3 | 4.5 | 5.0 | 5.5 | 5.92 | 5.98 | 7.0 | 7.67 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Most trials at S = 1 | 13 | 18 | 25 | 30 | 33 | 45 | 59 | 75 | 78 | 139 | 202 |
| Most trials at S = 0.75 | 5 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 | 17 | 24 | 30 |

### Which length counts

The length that matters is the one over which configurations are compared and the best
chosen. In this gate that is the joined walk-forward out-of-sample series, since the
deflated Sharpe is computed on it and the holdout candidate is chosen from it. So the
condition is on out-of-sample years, which G0.7's fold design sets, not on the raw
span. With an initial training window of t years:

| Window | Span | OOS after 1 year | after 2 years | after 3 years |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | 7.67 | 6.67 (116 trials) | 5.67 (66) | 4.67 (37) |
| H1 funding filter | 5.92 | 4.92 (42) | 3.92 (23) | 2.92 (13) |
| H2 | 5.98 | 4.98 (44) | 3.98 (24) | 2.98 (13) |

(trials supported at S = 1 in brackets).

### The caps, fixed

- **H1: 12 trials.** Its 7.67 years support far more at S = 1; 12 needs only 2.77
  years of out-of-sample returns.
- **H2: 12 trials.** Its 5.98 years support 78; 12 needs 2.77 years out of sample, and
  12 plus the whole reserve (18) needs 3.44.
- **Reserve: 6.** The total of 30 needs **4.30 years** of joined out-of-sample returns
  for every trial that can be chosen as the single holdout candidate. H2 and the
  funding-filter trial meet that only with a first training window of at most about 1.6
  years (5.92 - 4.30). If G0.7 needs more, the total cap is the "most trials" figure for
  the out-of-sample length left, cut from the reserve first: 25 at 4.0 years (a reserve
  of 1), 24 at 3.98 (none); below that the hypotheses' own caps fall too.
- Benchmark and random-sign control runs cannot be selected, so they do not count
  towards N for this rule, though the ledger records them.
- At S = 0.75 the split would not stand: H2's 5.98 years would support 17 trials in
  all. Adopting the stricter target is G0.7's call; this report fixes S = 1.

What the rule cannot do: it bounds the expected best Sharpe from noise, not the chance
of a lucky one, and it assumes the trials are tried once each. The ledger's N in the
deflated Sharpe, the PBO and the single holdout run are what guard the rest.

## Findings

Each with severity (what goes wrong if ignored) and confidence.

1. **High, confirmed in the archive's dates.** LUNAUSDT is two different coins: the
   original Terra until 2022-05-13 and the new Terra from 2022-05-31. A trend signal or
   return computed across the 17 missing days compares unrelated prices. H1 must treat
   the symbol as two instruments; the original continues as LUNCUSDT from 2022-09-09.
2. **High, confirmed.** A held pair can stop trading: LUNA (in the top five) collapsed
   and halted, FTT was suspended for ten months. G0.7 must fix how a position in a
   halted or delisted pair is valued and exited, or the backtest will invent an exit
   price.
3. **High, confirmed.** Spot archive files from 2025-01 onwards carry timestamps in
   microseconds, earlier ones in milliseconds. A loader assuming either would misplace a
   year of data. Handled and tested.
4. **High, confirmed.** At 28 of 93 month ends a top-five pair is younger than H1's
   120-day lookback, so its signal is undefined. Unless G0.7 fixes the treatment before
   the first trial, choosing it afterwards is a hidden degree of freedom.
5. **Medium, confirmed.** The universe rule is underspecified at its edges, and the top
   ten includes pairs a reader would not call coins: leveraged tokens (ETHBULL, ETHBEAR
   in early 2020), gold tokens (PAXG, XAUT in 2026) and a tokenised share (SNDKBUSDT in

6. **Medium, confirmed.** The rebalance at the 2025-12-31 close fills at the first

7. **Medium, confirmed.** The archive has outage gaps, off-grid bars and repeated rows
   (listed above). The loaders (G1.1) must fail on them rather than skip, and a strategy
   crossing one must say how; the four-hour trial and the 15-minute fill rule meet them
   most often, before 2020-03.
8. **Medium, confirmed.** 41 candidate perpetuals settle funding at other than 8-hour
   intervals for some or all of their history. Funding compared across coins or accrued
   over time must be per settlement as published, never the rate times three a day.

What this report cannot tell: whether any of these instruments carries an edge. It
measures only that the data exist, where they do not, and how long the development
window is.

## Reproduce

From `research/`, with `NORSE_DATASETS_DIR` set (or present in norse-stack's `.env`):

```bash
python -m norse_research.coverage universe   # list, mirror and rank every USDT pair
python -m norse_research.coverage sync       # mirror every candidate's series
python -m norse_research.coverage report     # write research/coverage/series-coverage.json
python -m pytest tests/ -q                   # includes the MinBTL known-answer test
```

Each step reuses files already mirrored and verified, so a rerun fetches only what is
missing. A run on a later date lists the archive afresh; the listing date of every
prefix is kept beside the mirror.
